Part VII · Chapter 30 · Which of this changes for your seat?

Position Three

It is minute fourteen and you are ahead. Not the team — you. You have more gold than the carry you have spent fourteen minutes standing in front of, and for the moment you have more than anyone on your own side, including the player whose gold the entire draft was built to protect.

Hold on to that, because it will not last, and the way it goes will not be a report on how well you chose. So the question is what the next three minutes are for. Not am I winning — the number in front of you will move whichever way the game goes, and the direction it moves is not a report on how well you chose.

What actually happened

Team Syntax vs Ilbirs Esports · Asgard Championship · match 8926599506 · patch 7.41e

Forty-three minutes and fourteen seconds, Radiant won, seven teamfights. What makes this game worth the chapter is that it contains both offlanes — two players in the same seat, in the same match, under the same patch, both of whom were ahead of the enemy carry they were laning against at minute ten.

Take the Radiant one first. At minute 10 the offlane hero had 4,477 gold and 47 last hits. The enemy safe-lane carry facing him had 2,736 and 34. That is a gap of +1,741 gold and +13 last hits against the hero whose farm the opposing draft exists to guarantee.

It goes further than the lane. From minute 6 through minute 14, that offlane hero held the highest gold of anyone on his own five — ahead of his mid, ahead of his carry. Meanwhile his side was 2,161 gold behind overall at minute 14. The richest player on the poorer team was the offlaner.

Stop here and commit before reading on

It is 14:00. You are the Radiant offlaner. Everything below is in the record at that moment, and nothing below it is.

YOU 5,793 gold · 67 last hits · Blink finished at 12:20 · two deaths THEM the carry you are laning against: 5,400 gold · 53 last hits LANE GAP +393 gold, +14 last hits four minutes ago it was +1,741 and +13 TEAM you have the highest gold of your five GAME your side is 2,161 gold behind overall

Commit in writing, before reading on:

  1. The objective. What the next three minutes are meant to buy — stated in a sentence that does not contain the word gold.
  2. The acceptable cost, as a quantity. Your own death is a legitimate line item here; write the number.
  3. The stopping condition — the observable event that means this has stopped buying anything.
  4. The missing information — what you would most want to know and do not.

Three answers all pass. One is an objective on the map. One is hold, keep farming, and here is the event that would change my mind — inaction with a stated condition is a decision, and the seat is not obliged to spend on a schedule. The third is the answer this chapter was written around: these two numbers do not establish that I am ahead in any way that matters, and here is what would settle it. If you take that one, name the further information. That is the whole of it.

What does not pass is keep doing what I am doing without a stopping condition, because there is nothing there to be right or wrong about later.

Read that table in either direction and it says nothing. It does not say the ten deaths were the better play — this book bans grading a decision by what came after it, and would be breaking its own rule in the seat chapter if it made an exception here. It does not say the tidier row was worse. It says the two rows do not order the same way as the result, so the row is not the evidence, and a player who has spent a season reading their own row after each game has been reading something else.

The reasoning

Observed. Every figure above: both minute-ten gaps, the rank series from 1st at minute 6 to 4th at minute 42, the sign change at minute 16, the team gold deficit at minute 14, both final rows, the two tower times, the fight at 16:27, and ten death timestamps.

Supported inference. For both offlaners in this game, the one quantity the record keeps about the seat — distance ahead of a single named opponent — was largest before minute fifteen and negative afterwards. And across the two of them, the final row does not order with the result.

Hypothesis. That the early advantage was converted into something. Something did happen: the same team's carry finished on 32,900 net worth and 500 last hits with two deaths in forty-three minutes, and did 10,690 damage to buildings, the highest in the game. Set that beside an offlaner who died ten times and it forms an attractive story. It is a story. The record contains no positions, no lane pressure, no attention, and no counterfactual, so it cannot connect one row to the other, and this chapter does not.

Unknowable. Whether either offlaner played well. What any other line would have produced. What either of them intended, at minute fourteen or at any other minute.

So the chapter cannot tell you that the deaths bought the carry's farm, and it is not going to imply it with a well-placed paragraph break. What it can establish is narrower and more useful:

The offlane's standing against its lane opponent is recorded directly, and it has a shelf life. Here it was +1,741 at minute ten and negative by minute sixteen — and the row went on reporting it, at the same size, for another twenty-seven minutes.

The direct comparison is not unique to this seat: the mid lane is measured against its opposite number in the same way, and rather more famously. What the record shows here is narrower — that for this offlaner the comparison held a large value early, crossed zero, and kept being displayed afterwards. A support has no comparable number at any point in the game, which is the contrast chapter 32 was written from.

Which produces the seat's characteristic mistake, and it is not the one the genre warns about. The warning is usually don't farm too much. The mistake in the record here is subtler: the number was still on the screen at minute twenty, and at minute thirty, and it was still the first thing a player in that seat would look at, long after it had stopped being about them.

What this seat changes

Part VII asks one question of each seat: how does it change what is scarce, what is visible, and which presumption you start from? Position three's answers:

AxisPosition three
What is scarce Not gold — this seat has a core's claim on it. What is scarce is the window in which your advantage is still yours. You are given a measurable lead over one specific opponent, early, and it decays whether or not you use it.
What is visible A great deal, and then very little. Early on the record holds a direct, checkable measurement of what you were sent to do — a support has nothing comparable at any point. Afterwards your row keeps reporting a race you have left, at the same size, with no marker for the moment it stopped applying.
Which presumption A falling rank inside your own five is not presumed to be a failure — and not presumed to be fine either. It is the expected shape for this seat, so it carries no information on its own. What needs an explanation is never the decline; it is what, if anything, happened elsewhere while it was declining.

Note which chapters this hands back to rather than re-teaching. Whether an advantage must be spent at all belongs to chapter 15, and detecting that an accumulated advantage has stopped producing progress is chapter 21's job in the endgame. This chapter's exclusive claim is smaller and about the seat: which two numbers describe you, and when they stop.

Where this stops applying. The fifteen minutes are this match, not a law. Drafts that leave the offlane farming safely, or that put the carry into a losing lane, move the crossover in either direction and there is no registered evidence here for a typical value. Read your own crossover; do not adopt this one.

It does not apply to a lane you are losing. Everything above starts from being ahead at minute ten, and a player who is behind is answering a different question — one this book puts in Part II rather than here.

And the professional gap is the wide one in this chapter. Ten deaths from an offlaner in a coordinated team can be paid for by four players who know what to do with what those deaths buy. In solo queue nobody has agreed to that trade, and a reader who takes the death count as permission has read this chapter backwards.

What this case does not establish. Anything at all about Archon–Legend offlanes. The bracket sample behind this book is drawn from OpenDota's public match feed, which reports duration, rank tier and result and no per-hero data of any kind — so there is no registered evidence here about when a bracket offlaner's lane advantage decays, whether it decays, or what any of them do about it. An inversion would look like this: a stated bracket at which holding the early lane advantage longer produces the better result, and holding it the same length produces a worse one. Nothing here can test that, and the block stays empty rather than being filled with reasoning.

What transfers, and what does not.

Transfers: the reading procedure — the two numbers that describe this seat, the fact that they expire, and the discipline of looking somewhere else once they have. So does the two-offlane comparison, which is a fact about what a row can report.

Does not transfer: minute sixteen, the ten deaths, the item timings, and the shape of the decay. Those are one game between five coordinated professionals and five others.

Next game

  1. At minute ten, read two numbers and write them down: your gold, and the gold of the carry you are laning against. The difference is the only direct measurement of this seat you are going to get.
  2. Name what the difference is for, in a sentence that does not contain the word gold — a tower, a camp you take away from them, a rune you can contest, a rotation you can now survive.
  3. Before you go, set the stopping condition out loud: the observable event that means the difference has stopped being worth spending.
  4. After minute twenty, stop opening the scoreboard to check your net worth position among your own five. It is answering a question you have already left. Read the enemy safe-lane hero's row instead — that is the one your first fifteen minutes were addressed to.
  5. At the end of the game, write one line describing something you did that appears nowhere in your own row. If you cannot find one, write that down too. It is the more informative result.

After the game

Open the replay at minute fourteen. Not at a death, and not at a fight — at the last minute your lane advantage was still large enough to be worth something.

The observable decision: what you did with the following three minutes.

The pass mark, set before you look: before minute fifteen you had named an objective that was not your own gold, and a maximum acceptable cost. You pass if the attempt achieved that objective inside that cost, or if you disengaged when the condition you named actually occurred. Holding deliberately, with a stated condition, passes on the same terms.

Objective: their bottom tier-one, or the camp behind it Cost: my own death, once — no cores Stop if: their mid appears on my half of the river Missing: whether their support has bought a sentry yet

What is not on the pass mark: your net worth, your rank in your team, and the result. All three are available and all three are the outcome, which chapter 01 grades nothing by. The two offlaners above make the case better than an argument can — one of these rows won the game and the other did not, and you cannot tell which from looking.

Do this once a game for ten games. What you are building is not a verdict on any single one; it is a record of whether you habitually spend the window, hold it past its expiry, or have never noticed it was there.

The seat labels are OpenDota's lane assignment, which is a judgement rather than a fact — roles in a professional game are fluid once the lanes break, and the chapter uses them only for the laning stage, where they are most reliable.

Chapter 30 is a pilot. It exists to test one structural question — whether the variation Part VII describes is indexed by seat or by something else that position five happens to sit at one end of.
